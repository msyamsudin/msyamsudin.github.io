Executive Summary

The Indonesian government has fundamentally altered the landscape of natural resource exports through the enactment of Government Regulation (PP) No. 24/2026. This regulation establishes PT Danantara Sumberdaya Indonesia (DSI) as the sole entity authorized to manage exports and determine international selling prices for strategic commodities, including coal, CPO, and ferroalloys. For PT Adaro Energy Indonesia Tbk (ADRO), one of the nation's largest coal producers, this represents a paradigm shift from a free-market export model to a state-controlled monopoly.

The primary objective of this policy is to eradicate under-invoicing and transfer pricing—specifically the practice of routing sales through intermediaries in Singapore to minimize tax liabilities 3, 5. While the government aims for optimized state revenue and transparency, the market has reacted with apprehension. ADRO faces a significant loss of "pricing power," potential margin compression, and increased operational rigidity. This report analyzes the phased implementation of the DSI mandate and evaluates the resulting financial and strategic risks to ADRO’s stock performance.

Detailed Analysis of the New Pricing Framework (PP No. 24/2026)

The introduction of PP No. 24/2026 marks a transition toward a "single-door" export system. Under this regime, PT DSI is not merely a regulator but a commercial entity acting as the exclusive gateway for strategic resources 1, 4. The government's logic is rooted in the belief that individual companies often manipulate prices to shift profits to low-tax jurisdictions, thereby depriving the Indonesian state of essential foreign exchange (DHE) and tax revenue 10.

The implementation is structured in three critical phases, each escalating the level of state control:

  1. The Oversight Phase (Starting June 1, 2026): In this initial stage, PT DSI acts as a verifier. Exporters like ADRO are still permitted to handle their own transactions, but they must report every sale to DSI. DSI then audits these prices against global benchmarks to ensure they are not being undervalued 3, 8.
  2. The Intermediary Phase (June – December 2026): During this transition, PT DSI begins to act as a middleman. While the companies still produce the goods, the contractual flow starts to shift toward the DSI entity 3, 4].
  3. The Monopoly Phase (Starting January 1, 2027): This is the most disruptive stage. PT DSI becomes the single exporter. All transactions must occur via DSI's integrated digital B2B platform. Private companies will no longer be able to export independently; they effectively become suppliers to DSI, which then sells to the global market 1, 10.

This systemic shift transforms the role of a mining company from a global trader to a domestic producer. For a company like ADRO, which has spent decades building a sophisticated global distribution network and long-term buyer relationships, this is a profound loss of autonomy.

Operational Impact on PT Adaro Energy Indonesia Tbk (ADRO)

The operational ramifications for ADRO can be categorized into three main areas: pricing power, profit margins, and financial flexibility.

Loss of Pricing Power and Autonomy

Historically, ADRO has leveraged its scale to negotiate favorable contracts and time its sales to coincide with market peaks. Under the DSI regime, this "pricing power" is transferred to the state. ADRO will no longer be the primary negotiator with international buyers; instead, PT DSI will determine the final selling price 1. This removes the company's ability to use strategic pricing as a tool for competitive advantage or market share expansion.

The Margin Dilemma: Cost-Plus vs. Back-to-Back

A critical point of contention is whether PT DSI will take a "cut" or margin from the transactions. There are two theoretical scenarios: * The High-Impact Scenario: If DSI employs a cost-plus or fixed-price model, they could potentially capture a portion of the profit margin that previously belonged to ADRO, significantly depressing the company's bottom line 9, 10. * The Low-Impact Scenario: Some reports suggest that for "established exporters" who already sell at fair international market prices, DSI may not take a marketing margin 8. Given ADRO's transparency and global standing, it is likely to fall into this category, which would mitigate the direct financial hit to its margins.

Repatriation and Financial Rigidity

The policy is designed to force the repatriation of all export profits back into Indonesia 5. For ADRO, this means a loss of flexibility in how it manages its cash flows and foreign exchange reserves. The ability to "park" funds in offshore entities or use intermediaries for strategic financial maneuvers is effectively eliminated 8. Furthermore, the transition to a digital B2B platform introduces "high-cost economy" risks, where administrative bottlenecks could slow down the speed of transactions, particularly in the volatile spot market 4, 6.

Impact on ADRO Stock Performance

The stock market typically hates uncertainty, and the DSI announcement provided a surplus of it. The immediate reaction was a sharp decline in the share prices of commodity-linked stocks.

Short-Term Market Reaction

ADRO's stock experienced a decline of 6.50% within five days leading up to May 25, 2026 9. This sell-off was driven by investors pricing in the risk of lower Average Selling Prices (ASP) and the loss of corporate agility. The broader trend saw commodity stocks leading the decline in the Jakarta Composite Index (IHSG) following the announcement 2.

Relative Risk Assessment

Interestingly, analysts note that ADRO's correction was more moderate compared to other peers like BYAN (Bayan Resources) or HRUM (Harum Energy) 7. This suggests that the market perceives ADRO as having a slightly more resilient structure or a better ability to adapt to the new regulations. However, the "overhang" remains: as long as the final details of the DSI pricing mechanism are unclear, the stock will likely trade at a discount 2, 9.

Strategic Paralysis

The uncertainty has extended beyond the stock price to corporate strategy. PT Adaro Andalan Indonesia Tbk (AADI), an affiliate of ADRO, has reportedly paused several strategic decisions while waiting for the "derivative regulations" (regulasi turunan) to be released 6. This "wait-and-see" approach indicates that the company cannot accurately forecast its future cash flows or ROI for new projects without knowing exactly how DSI will handle the pricing and margins.

Detailed Scenario Analysis

Scenario A: The Pass-Through (Back-to-Back)

In this scenario, PT DSI acts as a transparent conduit. They set the price based on the global index (e.g., Newcastle or ICI) and pass the payment directly to ADRO without taking a significant commission. * Strengths: Maintains the current revenue stream; prevents a collapse in net profit. * Weaknesses: ADRO still loses the ability to negotiate "premium" prices for specific coal grades. * Ideal Use Case: This is the most likely outcome for "blue-chip" exporters like ADRO who already comply with international standards 8.

Scenario B: The State Cut (Margin Capture)

Here, PT DSI operates as a traditional trading house, buying from ADRO at a "domestic price" and selling to the world at a "market price," pocketing the difference. * Strengths: Maximizes state revenue and DSI's balance sheet. * Weaknesses: Directly erodes ADRO's EBITDA and net income; likely to trigger a massive sell-off in ADRO shares. * Ideal Use Case: This would be applied to smaller, less transparent exporters who have historically manipulated prices 9.

Scenario C: The Strategic Pivot (Hilirisasi)

ADRO accelerates its transition from exporting raw coal to producing processed minerals or energy products (downstreaming/hilirisasi). * Strengths: Downstream products often fall outside the "strategic raw commodity" definition, potentially exempting them from DSI's monopoly. * Weaknesses: Requires massive capital expenditure (Capex) and carries high execution risk. * Ideal Use Case: The long-term survival strategy to regain autonomy from state-mandated pricing.

Shared Considerations for All Scenarios

Regardless of which pricing model PT DSI ultimately adopts, there are several "universal" impacts that ADRO must navigate:

  1. Digital Transformation Risk: The shift to a B2B digital platform is a double-edged sword. While it promises transparency, any technical failure or bureaucratic inefficiency in the DSI system becomes a bottleneck for ADRO's entire export pipeline 4.
  2. Regulatory Compliance Costs: The cost of auditing, reporting, and aligning internal systems with DSI's requirements will increase general and administrative (G&A) expenses.
  3. Geopolitical Shift: By centralizing exports, Indonesia is moving toward a "National Oil Company" (NOC) style model for coal. This may change how international buyers perceive the reliability and stability of Indonesian coal supplies.

Best For Verdicts

  • Best for State Revenue: Scenario B (Margin Capture). This allows the government to maximize every cent of the commodity boom.
  • Best for ADRO's Short-Term Stock Price: Scenario A (Pass-Through). If the market confirms that DSI will not take a margin from established players, the current "overhang" will disappear, and the stock could recover.
  • Best for ADRO's Long-Term Sustainability: Scenario C (Hilirisasi). By moving up the value chain, ADRO reduces its vulnerability to the whims of domestic regulatory shifts.

Conclusion: Final Answer to the Question

Does the PT Danantara Sumberdaya Indonesia (DSI) pricing rule impact ADRO? Yes, profoundly. The impact is predominantly negative to risky in the short and medium term.

The core of the issue is the transfer of sovereignty. ADRO is moving from a position of market leadership—where it controlled its own pricing and buyer relationships—to a position of a state supplier. The immediate impact on the stock has already been visible, with a 6.50% drop in share price as investors reacted to the loss of pricing power 9.

However, the severity of the long-term impact depends on the margin structure. If PT DSI treats ADRO as a "mapan" (established) exporter and does not take a transaction margin 8, the financial blow will be limited to administrative inefficiency and loss of flexibility. If DSI implements a margin-capture model, ADRO's profitability will be directly compromised.

Final Verdict for Investors: ADRO is currently in a state of "strategic paralysis" 6. Until the derivative regulations are released and the exact pricing mechanism (Back-to-Back vs. Cost-Plus) is confirmed, the stock will likely remain volatile and under pressure. The only definitive "hedge" for ADRO is to accelerate its downstreaming (hilirisasi) efforts to escape the raw commodity export trap.
